    Reading the map

    One circle per state, sized by its count of registered 503B facilities, the number inside. A dashed ring is a state with none. The small dots are the facilities themselves. The insets name the places government documents give as origins; no supply lines are drawn, because the documented routes are parcels through express hubs and drums through importers, not a line on a map.

      The court record: what is pursued, and what has held up

      Warning letters say what FDA thinks. Rulings, pleas and settlements say what held up in front of a judge. Read down the right-hand column for the pattern. Allegations in pending cases are not findings; none of this is legal advice.

      • Held up. FDA's shortage delistings, through two district courts and the Fifth Circuit. The intended-use test read from the whole record, in every plea. State consumer-protection law against "pharmaceutical grade" claims.
      • Defensible, on this record. A patient-specific prescription with a documented clinical difference. Bulk substance from an FDA-registered maker with a certificate of analysis, Green List origin for GLP-1 API. No equivalence claims. No research-use-only sales to patients.
      • Untested. No court has yet ruled on the research-use-only theory for peptides as such; the pleas were negotiated. The HHS draft policy has not been litigated, and the Hims referral to DOJ has produced nothing public.
