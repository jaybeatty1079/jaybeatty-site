Which peptide, and how does it reach you?

Pick one at the counter. The doors open, the cart climbs the track that today's US rules lay down for it through the Lebanon Valley in October, and twelve windows show how the compound is made, from the sequence on paper to the vial in your hand, and whether the rules permit each step. At the end you get the peptide's standing: where it sits under today's rules, with dates and sources. About two minutes.

The weather on the ride follows the peptide's standing. Sunny where there is oversight, an approved product or FDA or HHS tolerance, and human evidence behind it. Overcast where there is oversight but the human evidence is thin. Rain, a storm or a blizzard only where there is no oversight at all, graded by how little human evidence exists. How to read the ride

Twelve windows open as you climb, taking the four corners in turn, north-west, south-west, north-east, south-east, three times over. They tell how the compound is made: the sequence and where it came from, the raw materials, building the chain, cleaving the crude product, purifying it, the certificate, the border, the facility, formulation, fill and finish, labeling and release, and dispensing. The banner over each gate carries that step's words. Green means the rules permit the step, amber means with conditions, red means the gate is closed. A cart that stops at a gate, or gets shunted onto the research-use-only siding, is the answer.

Where the facts come from

Every status and date in a standing is a row in the federal register behind this tool: FDA's lists and letters, the Federal Register, Regulations.gov, the courts. The map holds the register, the 503B facilities and the documented supply routes; the glossary holds the words.

What it is not

Not legal advice, and not a statement that any peptide is safe or works. "Tolerated" means FDA or HHS says it does not intend to act while conditions are met. It is never approval. The hills are the Pennsylvania countryside the ride is set in; the equipment in the windows is drawn to type, not from any named plant, and the making of a peptide is told as the trade generally does it, not as any one maker does.
