How to read the ride

Three lifts, four gates each. Twelve windows open as you climb, one at a time and left and right in turn, and tell how the compound is made: the sequence and where it came from, the raw materials, building the chain, cleaving the crude product, purifying it, the certificate, the border, the facility, formulation, fill and finish, labeling and release, and dispensing. The panel along the bottom carries that step's words, and the banner over each gate repeats its name; the header of both is green where the rules permit the step, amber with conditions, red where the gate is closed, blue for an approved product. Press Space, or the Pause button, to stop the ride and read; press it again to go on. A cart that stops at a gate, or gets shunted onto the research-use-only siding, is the answer. The cart parks at the terminal after the twelfth gate and the standing comes up, coloured green, yellow, orange or red by evidence and oversight.

Where the facts come from

Every status and date in a standing is a row in the federal register behind this tool: FDA's lists and letters, the Federal Register, Regulations.gov, the courts. The map holds the register, the 503B facilities and the documented supply routes; the glossary holds the words.

What it is not

Not legal advice, and not a statement that any peptide is safe or works. "Tolerated" means FDA or HHS says it does not intend to act while conditions are met. It is never approval. The hills are the Pennsylvania countryside the ride is set in; the equipment in the windows is drawn to type, not from any named plant, and the making of a peptide is told as the trade generally does it, not as any one maker does.
