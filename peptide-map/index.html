Which peptide, and how does it reach you?

Pick one. The cart climbs the track that today's US rules lay down for it, each station shows what is permitted at that step, and the plate at the end gives you the facts with their dates and sources. About fifty seconds.

Maps public guidance as of the date above. Not legal advice. The HHS policy of 2026-10-30 is a draft under comment.

    How to read the ride

    Four stations on the climb: source, fill and finish, pharmacy, prescriber. Green means the rules permit the step, amber means with conditions, red means the gate is closed. A cart that stops at a gate, or gets shunted onto the research-use-only siding, is the answer.

    Where the facts come from

    Every status and date on the plate is a row in the federal register behind this tool: FDA's lists and letters, the Federal Register, the courts. The atlas plate holds the register, the 503B facilities and the documented supply routes.

    What it is not

    Not legal advice, and not a statement that any peptide is safe or works. "Tolerated" means FDA or HHS says it does not intend to act while conditions are met. It is never approval.
