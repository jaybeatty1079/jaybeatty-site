    Unit costs behind the levers (editable)

    SNF per day = MedPAC 2024 average payment per stay ($20,970) over the average stay (30.7 days). IRF = MedPAC 2024 average payment per stay. Home health = MedPAC 2024 average payment per 30-day period. Readmission = HCUP Statistical Brief 278, average cost of a Medicare 30-day readmission, 2018.       How TEAM pays

      Six steps from a surgery to a check, or a bill.

      CMS keeps paying claims as usual during the year. After the year closes it adds up what traditional Medicare spent on each episode and compares it with a target price. The difference, after a few adjustments, is what changes hands.

      01 · Trigger

      An anchor stay or procedure

      A traditional-Medicare patient has one of the five surgeries at your hospital. The episode runs from admission through 30 days after discharge and includes nearly all Part A and Part B spending in that window.

      02 · Target

      A regional target price

      CMS averages three baseline years of episode spending in your census division, trends it forward, and takes a 2% discount (1.5% for CABG and bowel). Then it adjusts for your patients' risk and your hospital's profile.

      03 · Compare

      Spend versus target

      (target − actual spend) × episodes for each category. Below target earns a positive reconciliation amount; above target produces a negative one. Categories with 30 or fewer baseline episodes cannot go negative.

      04 · Quality

      The composite quality score

      A positive amount is reduced by up to 10% when quality is weak: 10% × (1 − CQS/100). A negative amount shrinks when quality is strong: 15% × CQS/100 in Track 2, 10% × CQS/100 in Track 3.

      05 · Limits

      Stop-gain and stop-loss

      Track 1 caps gains at 10% of your aggregated target price and has no downside. Track 2 caps both sides at 5%. Track 3 caps both sides at 20%.

      06 · Settle

      The net payment reconciliation amount

      Positive, CMS pays the hospital. Negative, the hospital repays CMS. One reconciliation per performance year, with a later true-up for trend and post-episode spending.

      Methodology

      What the calculator does, and what it leaves out.

      Built to be checked. Every rule links to the CMS document it came from.

      Calculation order

      1. Regional target per MS-DRG = benchmark × prospective trend × (1 − discount), read from the CMS PY1 workbook (29 MS-DRGs × 9 census divisions).
      2. Category target = count-weighted regional target × your risk multiplier (normalization and retrospective trend assumed 1.00).
      3. Reconciliation amount per category = (target − average spend) × episodes. Low-volume categories floored at zero.
      4. Aggregate to the hospital. Track 1 sets a negative aggregate to zero.
      5. Quality adjustment per the CMS worked tables.
      6. Stop-gain and stop-loss applied as a share of the aggregated target price. Result is the estimated NPRA.
      7. Five-year view holds PY1 prices flat in standardized dollars, compounds your yearly spend change, and moves you to Track 3 in any year your chosen track is not available.
      8. Levers: savings per episode = (SNF share moved to home health × (SNF days × SNF per day − home health period)) + (SNF share kept × days cut × SNF per day) + (IRF share moved × (IRF stay − home health period)) + (readmission points cut × readmission cost) + (SSI points cut × SSI cost) + (VTE points cut × VTE cost). Average spend falls by that amount and steps 2 to 6 run again.
      9. Service-line tabs filter the charts and show each line's contribution. CMS reconciles at the hospital level, so the net payment stays a hospital number; a line's "effect on the hospital result" is the hospital NPRA with only that line's levers applied, minus the NPRA without them.
      10. Hospital prefill (any participant, and the six cases): MS-DRG volumes from the CMS Medicare Inpatient Hospitals by Provider and Service file for calendar 2024 (traditional Medicare inpatient discharges; cells under 11 suppressed; outpatient joint and spine episodes shown as a count but not priced). Spend versus target per category = that category's national post-acute share × (the hospital's price-standardized 30-day post-discharge spending per episode ÷ its state's, from the Care Compare Medicare Spending by Claim file, all conditions), capped at ±20%. The anchor stay is held at target because TEAM pays it at a fixed standardized MS-DRG price. Quality score = volume-weighted average of the hospital's national percentile on 30-day hip/knee and CABG readmission (proxy for the hybrid hospital-wide measure, which has no public scores yet), its PSI-90 percentile, and 50 for the THA/TKA patient-reported measure (not yet public). Lever starting points: hip/knee and CABG readmission rates read directly from Care Compare; other readmission defaults scaled by the hospital's 30-day readmission spending ratio; SNF share scaled by its SNF spending ratio (0.5 to 1.8×); SNF days by county SNF days per 1,000 versus national (0.7 to 1.4×); IRF share by county IRF spending per capita versus national (0.3 to 2×); VTE by the hospital's PSI-12 rate versus the national median (0.4 to 2×); major-bowel SSI by its NHSN colon SSI ratio (0.3 to 2.5×, when at least one predicted infection). Safety-net flag: Medicare SSI share above the 75th percentile of IPPS hospitals (8.7%, FY2027 impact file); rural: the impact file's geographic flag. Risk multiplier stays 1.00.
      11. Spend by setting uses the national TEAM shares of anchor facility, anchor Part B and post-acute spend by category (IAC, 2021 to 2023 claims) applied to your average spend.

      Not modeled

      • Your actual hospital-specific risk multiplier and normalization factor. CMS sends them in the preliminary target price report.
      • The 99th-percentile outlier cap on single episodes (already inside the CMS benchmark). Enter your average spend.
      • Episode cancellations, overlap with ACOs and other models, post-episode spending monitoring, beneficiary incentives, SNF three-day waiver effects.
      • Target prices for PY2 to PY5, which CMS has not published. PY1 regional prices are held flat. The FY2027 rule adds MS-DRG and APC update factors to the trend from PY1 (expected in late 2026) and, from PY2, builds the normalization factor from all three baseline years; neither is in the public workbook yet.
      • The hospital's actual TEAM performance. Prefilled spend and quality come from all-condition public measures and spending patterns, so they show where a hospital's post-discharge habits point, not its surgical episode results. Hospitals should replace them with their own numbers.
      • Sequestration. Medicare Advantage volume (TEAM is traditional Medicare only).
      • Where your readmissions originate. Only your claims feed can show whether they come from a particular SNF or home health agency. CMS provides TEAM participants with claims data on request; this tool prices what fixing it is worth.
