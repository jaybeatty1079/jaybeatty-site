MethodHow a dot gets its colour

Volumes. Each hospital's 2024 traditional-Medicare discharges in the 29 TEAM MS-DRGs, from the CMS inpatient-by-provider file, plus its outpatient joint services (APC 5115) as an upper bound.

Spend versus target. The hospital's 30-day post-discharge spending pattern from the Medicare Spending by Claim file, compared with its census division, moves each category's spend above or below the regional target price. On top of that, where CMS publishes a surgical signal for the hospital, it is added as dollars per episode: excess readmissions after hip and knee replacement and after bypass surgery (the HRRP predicted-minus-expected rate times the cost of a readmission), the hospital's discharge-weighted excess-readmission ratio across all six HRRP conditions applied to each remaining category's typical readmission rate, and excess colon infections for bowel surgery. For joint replacement, where CMS published its own 90-day hip/knee payment measure for the hospital (388 participants; the measure was retired from Care Compare in 2025, so it comes from the April 2025 archive and covers July 2020 to March 2023 episodes), that measure's ratio to the simple mean of hospital values in its census division replaces the inferred pattern outright (the division, not the $22,530 national figure, because TEAM prices against the region), because it already contains the post-acute spend and the readmissions.

Quality. The composite quality score is a proxy built the way CMS weights it: for the hospital-wide readmission measure, the hospital's percentile on its six-condition HRRP ratio among TEAM-eligible IPPS hospitals (617 participants; the hybrid hospital-wide measure CMS will actually use has no public scores yet, and the few without any HRRP condition fall back to their hip/knee and CABG ranks); its PSI-90 percentile; and 50 for the patient-reported outcome measure that has no public scores yet.

Track in 2027. Performance year 2 is the first year with mandatory downside. Safety-net hospitals can stay in Track 1 with no repayment through 2028, so they are shown amber if they run over target. Rural, sole community, Medicare-dependent and essential-access hospitals go to Track 2 with a 5 percent stop-loss. Everyone else is in Track 3 with a 20 percent stop-loss. The second scenario puts every hospital in Track 3.

What is held constant. The 2024 volumes, the 2024 spending pattern, the readmission ratios from the FY2026 HRRP cycle, and the PY1 target prices. CMS has not published PY2 prices or the 2027 risk coefficients. This is the answer to one question: if the data CMS has already published described 2027, who would be writing a cheque.

What to make of itReading the map honestly

It is a pattern, not a bill. The hospital-level numbers are estimates from proxies, and the calculator's own notes say the risk adjustment CMS applies can move a result by more than the margin on most dots. The map is reliable about direction and about clusters, not about the dollar on any one hospital.

Readmissions are the visible lever. The "of which readmissions" column is the share of a hospital's gap that its own excess-readmission ratios explain (the condition-specific HRRP ratio for joints and bypass, the six-condition ratio for the rest). For most repayers it is a minority of the gap; post-acute spending, chiefly skilled nursing days, is the larger driver. Where readmissions are the larger piece, the fix is inside the hospital's own discharge process.

Safety-net shielding matters. The amber dots are hospitals that would repay if they were two-sided. They are not penalised in 2027 under the designated-track scenario, but the shield ends after 2028, and the second scenario shows what the map looks like without it.
