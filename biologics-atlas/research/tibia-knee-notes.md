## 2. Thin evidence, stated plainly

- **Plateau void fill is the only trauma application with Level I head-to-head data**, and it has three of them (Russell 2008 CPC vs ICBG; CERTiFy 2020 Cerament BVF vs ICBG; six RCTs pooled by Cooper 2022 and Cochrane 2024). All say the same thing: synthetic fillers match or beat autograft on subsidence with less blood loss and no donor site. Every other calcium phosphate or TCP product borrows this class evidence; none of HydroSet, Pro-Dense, Vitoss or chronOS has its own plateau RCT.
- **rhBMP-2 in trauma is a 2002 result that later trials could not reproduce.** BESTT (unreamed nails) drove the PMA; Aro 2011 (reamed nails, the modern US standard) missed its primary endpoint with a numerically higher infection rate; Lyon 2013 (closed fractures, CPM carrier) was stopped for futility. The 2006 subgroup paper is what Medtronic still quotes.
- **Nonunion has no US-marketed product with Level I evidence.** The only RCT against autograft in tibial nonunion (Friedlaender 2001) belongs to OP-1, withdrawn in 2014. BMAC rests on Hernigou's dose-response cohort and a heterogeneous systematic review. DBM has no long-bone nonunion comparative trial at all. PRP's umbrella meta-analysis flips sign depending on how you pool. Teriparatide is case series.
- **Antibiotic-eluting fillers: strong single-arm series, no completed RCT.** McNally 2016 (96% eradication, n=100) and the 2022 meta-analysis (92%, n=917, no RCTs) are the spine of the class. The one RCT that read out, FORTIFY in open tibial fractures, was inconclusive (64% vs 66%). CONVICTION (n=220, France) and the Biocomposites genex G pivotal (n=139, single-arm historically controlled) are the next readouts.
- **Masquelet vs bone transport** is observational: pooled union 84% (tibia, 643 cases) vs 94.6% for distraction osteogenesis in infected defects, no significant difference, no RCT.
- **HTO filler**: the best recent evidence (2026 systematic review, 1,640 knees) says filler choice does not change outcomes and may add complications under 10 mm.
- **Cellular allografts, OssiGraft, exosome-type products**: zero trauma evidence. The atlas tiers them investigational for trauma and says why.
- **LIPUS**: TRUST (n=501) is negative for operatively treated tibias; the PMA fresh-fracture indication is limited to casted fractures.

## 3. Ownership and status changes worth remembering

- OP-1: Creative BioMolecules/Curis to Stryker Biotech (HDE 2001), FDA panel vote against OP-1 Putty PMA (March 2009), sold to Olympus (closed 2011-02-01, about USD 60M), Olympus exit announced 2014-02-28, US/Canada availability ended 2014-05-30, West Lebanon NH plant to Novo Nordisk (2014).
- Norian: Synthes subsidiary, criminal pleas 2009-2010, divestiture ordered, Synthes to J&J 2012.
- Orthovita (Vitoss, HydroSet XT) to Stryker 2011 (USD 316M). Wright Medical (Pro-Dense, Osteoset, Allomatrix) to Stryker 2020. Stryker therefore owns three overlapping trauma void fillers.
- Osteotech (Grafton) to Medtronic 2010 (USD 123M). Osteocel: Osiris to NuVasive 2008, NuVasive to Globus 2023. Harvest (BMAC) to Terumo BCT 2013. Exogen: Smith & Nephew to Bioventus 2012.
- Bonesupport: ended Zimmer Biomet US distribution and went direct (2018-2019); Cerament G De Novo 2022, open-fracture 510(k) 2024, NTAP 2025, DRG reassignment 2026; Cerament V De Novo pending.
- Bone Therapeutics became BioSenic (2022) and shelved ALLOB after the 2023 Phase IIb failure.
- J&J has announced a DePuy Synthes orthopaedics separation (2025), relevant to chronOS/DBX/ViviGen channel stability.

## 4. What a trauma orthobiologics hiring manager will challenge

1. **"Infuse is on-label for open tibia, so why don't trauma surgeons use it?"** Expect to answer with Aro 2011 (reamed nails, no benefit, infection signal), the 14-day window, the IIIA/B subgroup, payer policies limiting rhBMP-2 to the exact PMA indication, and the fact that RIA made staged autograft cheap. Bonus: the closed-fracture CPM program (Lyon 2013) was Pfizer/Wyeth's, not Medtronic's.
2. **"Cerament G failed FORTIFY. How did it get an open-fracture indication and an NTAP?"** The honest line: De Novo in osteomyelitis created regulation 888.3046; the open-fracture claim came through a 510(k) against that predicate with FORTIFY's safety data; CMS's Breakthrough alternative pathway for NTAP does not require efficacy data. That is a regulatory-strategy story, not a clinical one.
3. **"Where is the line on Stimulan in the US?"** "May be used at an infected site" is on the K141830 label; mixing antibiotics is EU/UK/Canada-only labeling and off-label in the US. Reps who blur this get companies warning letters.
4. **"Name one nonunion RCT for a product you can sell today."** There isn't one. The best answers are class-level (BMAC cohort data, the 2025 network meta-analysis) and the honest admission that the field runs on diamond-concept expert consensus.
5. **"Cellular allograft for nonunion?"** No data; 361 HCT/P rules bar osteoinductive claims; highest price per cc in the bag.
6. **"What does DRG bundling mean for your product?"** Everything in the OR is inside MS-DRGs 492-494 (lower extremity procedures), 539-541 (osteomyelitis) or the fracture DRGs; the only product-specific inpatient money in this whole category is Cerament G's NTAP and FY2027 DRG reassignment. Outpatient fillers are packaged. Stimulators (E0760/E0747) are the only separately paid nonunion product and they are DME, not biologics.
7. **"Masquelet or transport?"** No RCT; union rates overlap; the choice is driven by defect length (over 7 cm raises infection risk after IMT), soft tissue, and the surgeon's frame tolerance. PRECICE magnetic transport is the newest non-biologic competitor.
8. **"Volume?"** There is no US procedure count for any tibial fracture type; anyone quoting one is using a market report. Zura's 4.9% nonunion rate on 309,330 claims is the defensible number.
9. **"Why do you list PRP and teriparatide?"** Because surgeons ask; both are marked unproven/off-label and the JSON says so.
10. **Dates and numbers that are easy to get wrong:** Cerament G De Novo is DEN210044 (2022-05-17), not DEN210017; Infuse trauma PMA is P000054 (2004), spine is P000058 (2002); OP-1 HDE is H010002 (2001); Exogen PMA is P900009 (1994); Stimulan is K141830 (2015); BESTT PMID is 12473698 (not 12377902 as in the brief).

## 5. State of the market today (October 2026)

US trauma biologics in the tibia and knee are a commodity void-filler market with one growth franchise. Calcium phosphate cements, calcium sulfate/HA composites and beta-TCP fillers compete on handling and price for plateau and pilon voids, where Level I data say synthetics match autograft (Russell 2008, PMID 18829901; Hofmann/CERTiFy 2020, PMID 31809394; Cooper 2022, PMID 35285251). FDA cleared at least five more MQV fillers in 2025-2026 (K241555, K250346, K250521, K252797, K261008) with bench data only. The osteoinductive segment has shrunk: OP-1 is gone, Infuse's trauma indication is contested by its own later RCT (Aro 2011, PMID 21454742), and no BMP successor has a trauma program (Locate Bio is spine; Osteogrow is unregistered; Novadip is an orphan pediatric BLA path). Cell therapy in acute fracture failed with ALLOB (2023). The growth is in infection: Bonesupport's Cerament G, the only FDA-authorized antibiotic-eluting filler (DEN210044, 2022; K234008 open fractures, 2024), posted US sales of USD 18.3M in Q2 2025 (+84%) and drove the company to SEK 356M quarterly sales in Q2 2026 (+30% constant currency) with 33-36% full-year guidance, helped by an NTAP for open fractures (from 2025-10-01) and an FY2027 DRG reassignment (from 2026-10-01). Cerament V's De Novo (data package completed 2026-09-21) and Biocomposites' genex G pivotal (NCT07270380, 2026-2027) will decide whether this becomes a two- or three-player category. Everything else in nonunion still runs on autograft (RIA, ICBG), BMAC and DBM under the diamond concept, with no Level I product evidence and bundled DRG economics.
