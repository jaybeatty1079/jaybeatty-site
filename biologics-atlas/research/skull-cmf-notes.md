# Skull and craniomaxillofacial slice: research notes (2026-10-22)

Slice file: `research/skull-cmf.json` (11 applications, 22 products, 46 pairings, 54 evidence rows). Region id `skull-cmf`; subregions `cranium`, `midface-orbit`, `mandible`, `dental-alveolar`. Sources: openFDA 510(k) and PMA queries run 2026-10-22, 510(k) summary PDFs (K161447, K253883, K251959, K253854 read from the FDA text layer; K060443, K032366, K043280, K123633 are scanned and could not be read without OCR), PubMed metadata for every PMID cited, ClinicalTrials.gov records, company product pages, FDA recall database.

## What the slice says in one paragraph

Below the dura there is no biologic story; above it there is a small one. The cranial "biologics" are self-setting calcium phosphate and hydroxyapatite cements, beta-TCP, bioactive glass and two 3D-printed PCL scaffolds, all cleared by 510(k) under product code GXP (which the FDA still names "Methyl Methacrylate for Cranioplasty", 21 CFR 882.5300, Class II) and all capped at 25 cm2 (4 cm2 for Stryker DirectInject). They fill burr holes, craniotomy kerfs and small or contour defects. Every large defect is either the patient's banked bone flap (15 to 20 percent resorption, about 7 percent infection, reoperation odds 1.9 times synthetic) or a custom PEEK, titanium or PMMA implant, which is hardware. The only labeled growth factor in the whole region is Infuse for sinus and extraction-socket augmentation (PMA P050053, 2007-03-09, 160-patient pivotal RCT), plus GEM 21S for periodontal defects (P040013, 2005). Everything else rhBMP-2 does in the face (cleft, mandible, calvarium) is off-label and in children the label explicitly says it has not been studied.

## What I could not verify

1. **BoneSource current status.** No discontinuation notice found, but it is absent from Stryker's US CMF pages (which list HydroSet and DirectInject) and has no 510(k) activity since K043334 (2005). Recorded as `off-market`, `unverified: true`. A Stryker CMF rep would know in one call.
2. **HydroSet cranial labeling.** The cranial indication (burr holes, contiguous craniotomy cuts, other cranial defects, craniofacial contour) sits on K060763 "Stryker Injectable Cement" (GXP, 2006-09-22). HydroSet XT (K161447, 2016) is an MQV orthopedic bone void filler whose indications list craniofacial among skeletal sites but do not name burr holes or craniotomy cuts. I could not find a 25 cm2 ceiling on either Stryker page or 510(k) summary; the product may be the only cement without one, or it is in the IFU I did not retrieve. Flagged as `challenge` on the product.
3. **Mimix US availability.** Zimmer Biomet's US CMF page lists Mimix and Mimix QS under neurosurgery and the EU page is live, but no 2024 to 2026 US brochure was found and GUDID still names Biomet Microfixation as labeler. `unverified: true`.
4. **Norian CRS in the 2026 DePuy Synthes catalog.** The pass-2 log found a Norian Putty leaflet dated September 2026; whether the CRS cranial SKUs (K060443, K060445) and Norian Reinforced (K102018) are all still sold was not confirmed. The 1998 K973789 Norian Corp clearance is the first cranial repair system of record.

6. **Abyrx ownership.** A web result describes a $450 million transaction creating MedEdge Holdings as owner of Abyrx and Kairuku; I could not date or source it to a press release. Orthocon, Inc. is the 510(k) holder for every Montage and Permatage clearance with FDA letters addressed in care of Abyrx, Stamford, CT. SurgiCor was the announced US distributor (2014, 2017). Acumed and OsteoMed host Montage 510(k) PDFs on their sites, which suggests a CMF distribution relationship I could not confirm.
7. **TnR Cranial Implant US distribution.** Cleared 2026-07-30 (K251959) with Osteopore as predicate; no US distributor named; the "20,000 cases in Korea" figure is the company's own.
8. **Osteopore US distributor.** A non-exclusive two-year US agreement with Bioplate (also spelled Biolate in one report) for cranial and neurosurgery is reported without a date. Zimmer Biomet's exclusive deal (July 2024) is EMEA and APAC only.
9. **Bonalive Maxillofacial launch.** K253883 (Special 510(k), 2026-05-14) renames the 2007/2019 dental granules with a new applicator and gamma sterilization; no press release, US dental distributor or launch date found. Bonalive's news page returned a 2021 snapshot.
10. **OsteoFab / Oxford Performance Materials.** Company site is live (South Windsor, CT, copyright 2024) but shows no news; the 2013 Biomet Microfixation exclusive distribution deal for cranial implants is the last public channel statement. Hardware only, so it only matters for the adjacent note.
11. **Kryptonite.** The FDA record is a Class 2 voluntary recall (Z-1496-2012, 3,100 kits, terminated 2015-07-14), not a formal withdrawal. Doctors Research Group's corporate status after 2015 was not checked. The Cureus infection cohort (RR 42) has no PMID in the PubMed index; cited by URL.
12. **GEM 21S channel.** Osteohealth (Luitpold) marketed it historically and Luitpold's Shirley, NY plant made the rhPDGF syringe; current distributor not confirmed.
13. **Cranioplasty volume.** No single US annual count exists. Two NIS series disagree by more than two-fold (49,305 adult cranioplasty encounters 2012 to 2014 vs 105,714 adult admissions 2002 to 2017) because of code definitions. Decompressive craniectomy totals exist only for stroke (124,718, 2006 to 2016) and severe TBI (23,743, 2004 to 2014). Craniosynostosis repairs: KID 2012 about 1,600 to 1,800 infants; NSQIP-P 11,279 over 2012 to 2021 (a sample, not a census).
14. **MS-DRG 023/024.** The brief named 023 to 024 (craniotomy with major device implant or acute complex CNS principal diagnosis). The Zimmer Biomet coding guide maps cranioplasty to 025 to 027; whether a custom implant case groups to 023/024 depends on the ICD-10-PCS device value and I did not verify grouper logic. 15. **DBX CMF labeling.** The J&J MedTech page confirms a DBX CMF product line (putty, paste, mix, strip, DBX Inject) but the IFU was not retrievable electronically; the 361 status and MTF processing are carried over from the spine slice.
16. **NovaBone cranial use.** No NovaBone 510(k) carries a cranial (GXP) indication; the only cranial comparison is an Ain Shams University thesis. Tiered investigational and off-label.

## Thin evidence a CMF hiring manager will challenge

- **Hydroxyapatite cement cranioplasty evidence is almost all retrospective and brand-agnostic.** The best numbers are class-level: 1,983-patient meta-analysis (infection or explantation 7.5 percent for large defects, 1.5 percent retrosigmoid) and the 2025 custom-implant review (porous HA 6.7 percent infection, 5.3 percent explantation). Nobody has run a cement-versus-cement trial, and the one cement-versus-custom-implant RCT (NCT03841643, n=20, Belgium) is unpublished.
- **Pediatric contour cranioplasty with cement looks worse than marketing suggests.** Norian in 20 children: 59 percent infection; Gilardino 46 patients: 26 percent complications with onset at a mean 20 months; Emory 78 children: 6 removals. The 2023 CHOP syndromic series is the best recent number (9.5 percent infection) and it used a mean 135 cm2 of onlay, well beyond the 25 cm2 label. Expect the question "why would I use cement over fat grafting or a custom implant in a child."
- **The 25 cm2 label ceiling is universal and universally exceeded.** Montage XT, Norian, Mimix, chronOS and QuickSet Mimix all carry it. HydroSet's page does not show it. TnR's 510(k) has no ceiling at all despite being a PCL-TCP block.
- **rhBMP-2 in alveolar cleft is Level I in form and low-certainty in substance.** Six RCTs, none large (n 16 to 19 in the Brazilian trials), all with uncertain or high risk of bias per the 2021 umbrella review; bone volume equal, bone height lower, operative time and stay shorter, facial swelling the characteristic complication (37.5 percent in Alonso 2010). The CHLA critical-volume paper (885 mm3) is the most useful clinical number in the slice. The US RCT (NCT04234971) has a cost primary endpoint, not healing.
- **rhBMP-2 in the mandible is case series.** Herford and Boyne (14 patients, all healed) versus Carter (2 of 4 continuity defects failed). The 2019 PRS Global Open review says the evidence fails to support it for mandibular reconstruction, and the label contraindicates use near a resected tumor, which is the usual indication.
- **The head-and-neck swelling warning is a spine artifact.** The FDA's July 2008 notification concerned cervical spine use (38 reports). Medtronic's dental ISI page lists pregnancy, malignancy, infection, hypersensitivity and skeletal immaturity, not airway swelling. The CMF swelling literature is the cleft RCTs and the 2026 systematic review (facial edema the commonest rhBMP-2 complication). Do not conflate the two.
- **Cellular allografts in dentistry rest on one 11-subject split-mouth trial** (Osteocel Plus, histologic vital bone at 14 weeks). Trinity Elite has no dental study.
- **Orbital and midface fractures have no biologic product paired**, which is correct: the slice says so and names the hardware. A manager will want that stated rather than padded.
- **Montage has eight GXP and MQV clearances in three years and zero clinical publications.** The January 2026 XT cranial clearance was a 30-day Special 510(k). The registered flap-fixation study is for an unlabeled use.
- **Kryptonite is the cautionary tale for the GXP code**: cleared in 2009 as substantially equivalent to PMMA on bench data, 49 percent unpredictable expansion, RR 42 for infection in one hospital, recalled 2012.
- **Tissue-engineered mandible constructs are not a market.** EpiBone's six-patient IND study has had an unknown status since May 2022; the strongest 2025 work (Nat Commun patient-matched permanent scaffold) is in sheep.

## State of the market today (sources in the slice)

Cranial void fill is a small, mature, direct-sales market owned by the three CMF franchises: Stryker CMF (HydroSet, DirectInject; BoneSource gone from the catalog), DePuy Synthes CMF (Norian CRS and Reinforced, chronOS, DBX CMF) and Zimmer Biomet CMF (Mimix, plus Osteopore PCL outside the US). The product that is actually moving is not a cement but a category: 3D-printed resorbable PCL scaffolds, with Osteopore's Zimmer Biomet deal (July 2024) and T&R Biofab's US clearance (July 2026) using Osteopore as predicate. The only genuinely new US entrant is Abyrx/Orthocon, which has filed eight clearances since 2023 and is testing Montage Flowable for cranial flap fixation (NCT07103330, n=100, start 2026), the same unmet need RevBio's TETRANITE adhesive is chasing under an IDE. Large-defect cranioplasty is a hardware market (PEEK, titanium, PMMA, sonolucent PMMA) selling against the 15 to 20 percent resorption rate of the autologous flap, and the FDA cleared at least ten custom cranial implant 510(k)s in 2024 to 2026. In the face, plates win every fracture and the fibula flap wins every continuity defect; biologics enter only in the alveolus, where Infuse holds the region's sole growth-factor label (sinus and socket, 2007) on a 160-patient RCT and is used off-label in cleft children on six small RCTs that show equivalence to iliac crest with more swelling. No product in this region has an NTAP, a pass-through code or a Level I cranial trial, and the FDA's product code for every cranial cement still reads "methyl methacrylate".
