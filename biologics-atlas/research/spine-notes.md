## Corrections to the brief

1. **Kuros/MagnetOs was not acquired by Globus.** Globus's 2025 deal was Nevro ($250M, closed Q2 2025); the Globus FY2025 10-K lists no Kuros transaction. Kuros is still an independent SIX-listed company (2025 revenue USD 146.1M, +72%). What did happen: a five-year exclusive sales-agency agreement with Medtronic's spinal division (announced 7 Jan 2025) covering defined US spine geographies, with Kuros keeping contracts and revenue recognition. The rumor probably comes from Kuros being the obvious next spine-biologics acquisition target.
2. **Locate Bio's LDGraft trial is ALIF, not TLIF/PLF.** RESTORE (NCT06462729) is a 40-patient, single-level ALIF dose-finding first-in-human study (Australia + three US sites after IDE approval, Sept 2025). The Breakthrough designation is for single-level ALIF L3-S1.
3. **Osteogrow's human trials are PLIF and TLIF, not posterolateral fusion.** The posterolateral work is rabbit and sheep.
4. **"Isto/Integra history" for Accell/Progenix conflates two companies.** Accell and Progenix came from IsoTis OrthoBiologics (Irvine, CA) -> Integra (2007) -> SeaSpine spin-off (2015) -> Orthofix (merger, Jan 2023). Isto Biologics (St. Louis; Magellan BMAC, InQu, Fibrant) is unrelated. Progenix's current status was not confirmed.
5. **The three PMA-approved spine bone grafts are now Infuse, i-FACTOR and PearlMatrix.** Two of the biggest regulatory events in the slice happened in the last 16 months: PearlMatrix PMA (23 Jun 2025; expanded to ALIF/PLIF/OLIF/LLIF 13 Jan 2026) and Infuse one- and two-level TLIF (approved 13 Feb 2026, announced 17 Feb). Any memory-based summary of "Infuse is only on-label for ALIF" is stale.

## Where the evidence is thin (and the JSON says so)

- **Cellular allografts (Trinity, Osteocel, ViviGen, OsteoAMP, Bio4, map3, OsteoVive, OssiGraft).** The only systematic review (Darveau 2021, PMID 34062506) found 12 studies of 6 products, all single-center or non-comparative, 7 with industry conflicts, and concluded there is insufficient evidence to recommend them over cheaper materials. The one matched comparison (Zakko 2023) showed ViviGen no better than DBM in ACDF. The rat model (Lin 2020) ranked Trinity ELITE far above Osteocel Pro and ViviGen, which Orthofix uses in selling and which competitors dismiss as animal data. Payer policies (e.g., FEP 8.01.52, Jan 2026) still call all of them investigational for spine fusion. Two trials will change this: MagnetOs Flex Matrix vs Trinity ELITE (NCT05037968, n=100, Dec 2026) and Corewell's ViviGen vs local autograft PLF RCT (NCT03733626, completed June 2024, unpublished).
- **DBMs.** The only RCT-grade human data are 20 years old (Cammisa 2004, 52% vs 54% side fusion) and a 19-patient Korean intra-patient study (Chang 2021). DBM potency varies by donor and the SeaSpine-funded rat study (Russell 2020) shows brand-to-brand differences that no one has tested in humans.
- **Synthetics other than MagnetOs.** Vitoss evidence is three single-surgeon series; Mastergraft, Signafuse, NovaBone, Fibergraft and Magnifuse have no published spine comparative study. The beta-TCP RCT (Dai 2008) used an unnamed Chinese beta-TCP. Actifuse's RCT is n=19.
- **MagnetOs.** The one RCT (Stempels 2024) is strong in design (100 patients, intra-patient, CT-scored) but the autograft side fused only 47%, which is far below what surgeons expect and makes the 79% look dramatic. Read it as "MagnetOs is at least as good as autograft in the gutter," not as a 32-point advantage over clinical reality. The "osteoinductive" and NeedleGrip/M2-macrophage language is marketing; FDA cleared it as an osteoconductive void filler.
- **PearlMatrix.** Superiority on composite clinical success (55.5% vs 37.5%) comes partly from a control arm that fused only 58.5% by strict CT at 24 months with ~60% high-risk patients. The January 2026 Cerapedics release itself notes a higher rate of secondary surgical interventions with PearlMatrix than autograft in TLIF. The ALIF/PLIF/OLIF/LLIF expansion was granted without approach-specific trials.
- **Infuse.** The 2026 TLIF approval rests on an unpublished 493-patient adaptive RCT; everything public is from Medtronic. The YODA reanalyses (Fu 2013; Simmonds 2013) and Carragee 2011 remain the reference points for safety: no clinical advantage over ICBG in lumbar fusion, cancer RR 3.45 at 24 months on low event counts, cervical swelling/airway events, retrograde ejaculation in ALIF. Amplify (rhBMP-2/CRM, the Dimar 2009 product) was never approved.
- **Osteogrow.** The EU register results (posted Feb 2026) show composite success and bilateral bridging in single digits for every arm including iliac crest; the sponsor says the definition was too strict. The numbers as extracted look implausible and should be re-read from the register before quoting.
- **VCF resorbable fill.** The only RCT of a calcium phosphate cement in kyphoplasty (Blattert 2009) went against it (9 cement failures in burst fractures vs 0 for PMMA). Cortoss is noninferior to PMMA but is a nonresorbable bioactive composite, so calling it a biologic is a stretch.
- **Nonunion rates for lateral fusion and SI joint fusion** are not pooled anywhere I could verify; both application records are flagged `unverified`.

## Things a spine biologics hiring manager would challenge

1. "Why do you list Infuse as established off-label in PLF and deformity?" Because that is where the volume is (Cahill 2009: 25% of all fusions used BMP by 2006; Bannwarth 2021: 74% of ISSG deformity cases at ~24 mg). The JSON keeps on_label false and cites the safety literature; it does not pretend the usage is not there.
2. "Osteocel Plus and Pro are the same product?" No; Pro is a later formulation with different viability processing. One record covers both because the evidence base (Eastlack 2014, Ammerman 2012, the NuVasive cohorts) is all Osteocel Plus.
3. "Trinity Evolution vs Trinity ELITE?" Same cell-containing cancellous base; ELITE adds demineralized fibers for handling. All the published clinical data are Evolution; the ongoing comparative trials use ELITE.
4. "MTF supplies DBX to DePuy and Trinity to Orthofix; who else?" Verified: Orthofix (Trinity, FiberFuse, Legacy DBM; through 2032; $51.7M marketing-service fees in 2024, ~31% of Orthofix Biologics revenue; MTF is single-source) and DePuy Synthes (DBX; inferred from labeling history, not from a fetched contract). The Premier GPO agreement (2021) and VA distribution (Academy Medical) are MTF's own channels. Other private-label relationships were not verified.
5. "Reimbursement is not just bundled." Correct at the margins: +20931/+20938 structural grafts can be physician-payable; outpatient ASC fusion packaging differs; SI-BONE got transitional pass-through for iFuse Bedrock Granite and NTAP for iFuse TORQ TNT (hardware, not biologics). No spine biologic has NTAP or pass-through that I found; PearlMatrix's coding guide could not be read.
6. "You are missing X." Known omissions: Cellentra, Fibrant, VIA Graft, nanOss (Xtant), OsteoSponge/OsteoSelect (Xtant), PUREBONE and Reficio (Bioventus), Strand/FiberFuse/Legacy (Orthofix/SeaSpine), Optium (LifeNet), AttraX (predicate to MagnetOs), Bioventus's Signafuse clinical program if any, Novosis/CGBIO, OsteoFlo/SurGenTec, Altapore (Baxter). The brief asked for 20-30 products; 31 are included and the omitted ones are mostly extenders without spine-specific data.

## State of the market today (Oct 2026)

The US spine biologics market is still dominated by the same four hardware companies that control the spine implant market, each with a biologics bag built to travel with its cages: Medtronic (Infuse, Grafton, Magnifuse, Mastergraft, plus MagnetOs as Kuros's sales agent since January 2025), Globus (Osteocel after the 2023 NuVasive acquisition, plus Harvest autologous), DePuy Synthes (DBX, ViviGen, chronOS, FIBERGRAFT) and Stryker (Vitoss, Bio4, Cortoss), with Orthofix (Trinity ELITE via MTF, Accell/Strand via SeaSpine) and Bioventus (OsteoAMP, Signafuse) as the mid-tier and Xtant, Ossium and the tissue banks underneath. Three things changed the shape of the market in 2025-2026. First, the PMA tier doubled: Cerapedics' PearlMatrix (June 2025, expanded Jan 2026) and Medtronic's Infuse TLIF label (Feb 2026) mean the largest lumbar fusion category now has two on-label osteoinductive-class products for the first time, with Level I data on each, which will pressure 361 cellular allografts that have never run a comparative trial and are already labeled investigational by payers. Second, Kuros grew 72% to USD 146M in 2025 on a single RCT and a Medtronic channel deal, proving that a synthetic with one good intra-patient trial can take share from allograft; its Flex Matrix vs Trinity ELITE head-to-head (NCT05037968) reads out around the end of 2026. Third, the next osteoinductive wave is early and small: Locate Bio's low-dose encapsulated rhBMP-2 (first-in-human ALIF, 2024-2027), Bone Biologics' NELL-1 (30-patient Australian pilot, data Q4 2027), Genera's rhBMP6 (EU phase II with disappointing posted fusion numbers), and DePuy's post-market bioactive-glass study, none of which will reach a US label before 2029. Intradiscal cell therapies (Mesoblast, DiscGenics, VIVEX) are on parallel 2027 readouts and are not fusion products. Sources: Medtronic 17 Feb 2026 release; Cerapedics 23 Jun 2025 and 13 Jan 2026 releases; Kuros FY2025 results (GlobeNewswire, 10 Mar 2026) and OrthoWorld/MassDevice coverage of the Medtronic agreement (7 Jan 2025); Globus FY2025 10-K; Orthofix FY2024 10-K; Bioventus FY2024 10-K; Xtant FY2024 10-K; ClinicalTrials.gov records cited in `spine.json`.

## Pass 1 (2026-10-22): SI-joint graft products

Data changes are in `research/pass1-patch.json` (one application patch, six new product records), not applied to `spine.json` directly, per the coordinator's instruction to avoid a concurrent-edit race.

### What the segment actually looks like

- **Lateral / posterolateral transfixing implants carry no branded biologic.** iFuse, iFuse-3D, iFuse TORQ and Bedrock Granite (SI-BONE), SI-LOK and SI-LOK SELECT (Globus), FIREBIRD SI (Orthofix, K210667), Rialto (Medtronic, re-cleared K251395 in June 2025), Siconus and the Camber SI implant (K162121, K203503), Liberty-SI (Spinal Simplicity), SiLO TFX (Aurora, K221047), TransLoc 3D (CornerLoc, K232878), Catamaran and the new SImmetry+ (Tenon) are all 510(k) titanium. Their product pages describe fusion through porous surfaces and bone the implant harvests itself (iFuse-3D "fenestrated design to self-harvest bone" in the 10-K; FIREBIRD "radial fenestrations ... self-harvesting of autograft"; SI-LOK "optional graft slot"; Rialto IFU "autograft and/or allograft may be placed in conjunction"). None names Trinity, Osteocel, a DBM or any other brand for the SI joint. The brief's "Trinity with FIREBIRD SI" and "Osteocel with SI-LOK" could not be confirmed on orthofix.com or globusmedical.com and are left unpaired. The INSITE (NCT01681004), iMIA (NCT01741025) and SALLY (NCT03122899) trials used no added graft; SALLY's independent CT read gave 77% bridging at 12 months and 90% at 5 years (Patel 2020 PMID 32607011; Patel 2024 PMID 39344079).
- **Posterior intra-articular products are the biologic.** Six families of machined structural cortical allograft, regulated as 361 HCT/Ps (homologous use), are sold into interventional-pain practices and ASCs: PainTEQ LinQ (plus the 2026 cortical-piercing TRAQ), SI-BONE iFuse INTRA V / INTRA X (2024), Aurora SiLO (2021), CornerLoc grafts (cortical graft plus a DBM sponge), Omnia PsiF allograft, and Vyrsa Pro (Vyrsa -> Nevro Nov 2023 -> Globus Apr 2025). PainTEQ's clinician page says in plain words that LinQ and TRAQ are "regulated solely under Section 361" and are "not FDA-cleared or FDA-approved medical devices"; SI-BONE's FY2025 10-K says INTRA/INTRA X are "manufactured and distributed by a third-party as HCT/P products" from "the tissue bank from which we obtain these products". **No vendor names its tissue bank.** That is the one thing the brief asked for that stayed closed.
- **The posterior segment is re-metallizing.** Omnia replaced its allograft with the titanium PsiF DNA (510(k) May 2025, launched Jan 2026); Spinal Simplicity's Patriot-SI (May 2024) and Camber's SI implant must be paired with a transfixing screw; CornerLoc added TransLoc 3D; Aurora added SiLO TFX; SI-BONE is starting a 120-patient study of the titanium cortical-piercing iFuse INTRA Ti (NCT07565545). The reason is coverage, below.

### Evidence (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov)

- Only posterior allograft dataset of any size: SECURE (NCT04423120; Calodney 2024 PMID 38524688), prospective single-arm, 16 sites, 122 implanted, composite responder 73.5% at 12 months, no CT fusion endpoint. Supporting retrospective series: Bovinet 2025 (PMID 40832586, n=258), Ampomah 2025 (PMID 40828365, n=260, AEs 1.9%), Sayed 2021 (PMID 34703300, n=50), Kaye 2024 (PMID 39087970; LSU, 72 fusions, 83% LinQ / 17% PsiF, the only series not authored by company consultants). PsiF allograft: Lynch 2022 registry (PMID 36420375, n=57). The 2025 systematic review (Gill, PMID 40539775) found 12 studies, 2 prospective, 0 randomized, heterogeneity too high to pool. First RCT of a posterior allograft is LINQT (NCT07558278, LinQ vs conservative care, n=200, started Jul 2026). Chin 2025 (PMID 40383200) is a 276-patient RCT of supervision models, not devices, using posterior-oblique titanium screws plus an unnamed "synthetic bioactive glass flowable" biologic.
- Grades assigned: LinQ IV, PsiF IV, iFuse INTRA V, SiLO V, CornerLoc V, Vyrsa Pro V; all tier "emerging". Nobody has a fusion rate for a posterior allograft; the only CT-graded graft-based SI series retrieved is SImmetry decortication-plus-graft (lateral), 94% bridging at 24 months.

### Coding and coverage (verified)

- CPT 27279 (2015, device-intensive) and 27278 (Category I from 1 Jan 2024, replacing Category III 0775T of 2022-2023). 2026 AMA revision: 27279 = transarticular and/or intra-articular devices that pierce the ilium/sacrum cortices; 27278 = intra-articular devices without cortical piercing (SI-BONE Feb 2026 facility guide; PainTEQ page; NASS SpineLine Mar/Apr 2024). 2026 CMS unadjusted: HOPD APC 5116 $17,914 for both; ASC $14,518 (27279) and $15,048 (27278), both device-intensive; physician 27279 $762, 27278 $441 facility / $13,834 office-based lab (SI-BONE 10-K). Inpatient (rare): 0SG734Z/0SG834Z group to FY2026 MS-DRG 450/451 Single Level Spinal Fusion Except Cervical with/without MCC, $38,782 / $23,507. C1889/C1776 packaged.
- Medicare LCDs: CGS L39802 (KY, OH), Noridian L39810/L39812 (JE/JF; JF retired 23 Oct 2025 into the JE document) and Palmetto L39797 (JJ/JM), effective 17 Apr 2025 (Noridian 16 Feb 2025), all state: MI arthrodesis of the SIJ "WITHOUT placement of a transfixation device is NOT considered medically reasonable and necessary". Noridian's evidence review quotes ISASS calling posterior MI SIJF "unproven and ... not recommended". SI-BONE's 10-K: three MACs "finalized LCDs precluding coverage for CPT 27278" and this "affected demand for INTRA X". NGS/Wellpoint L36406 (revised 1 Apr 2026) and WPS L36000 (rev. 27 Jun 2024) still define the covered procedure as "percutaneous insertion of a metallic device across the SIJ"; no Novitas or First Coast SI fusion LCD was found. ACR, ASPN and NANS asked for withdrawal (Feb 2025); the LCDs stand as of Oct 2026. The L39383 number in the brief is CGS's SI joint injections LCD (the fusion LCD cross-references it), not the fusion policy. iFuse Bedrock Granite has hospital-outpatient/ASC transitional pass-through from 1 Jan 2025 and iFuse TORQ TNT an NTAP (hardware).

### SI-BONE figures (FY2025 10-K, EDGAR, filed 2026)
